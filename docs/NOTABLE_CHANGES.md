# Notable Translation Changes

This page collects examples of changes in the patch. It is not a complete changelog. The English release often rewrote jokes, cultural references and character speech, and in some places changed who did what or what a line meant. The Japanese script was checked directly for the examples below.

## Major mistranslations and reversals

- A scene that calls for **thirty or forty** giant leaves (`三、四十枚`) said **three or four** in English. The missing decimal place changes the scale of the joke completely.
- Towa's `私のために戦わないで！` means **"Don't fight over me!"** The English line said **"Do it for me!"**, reversing the plea.
- The contaminated-sugarcube plot now distinguishes **poisoning** from **addiction**. The English release called the poisoning victims addicts while dropping the explicit statement that **Adan's Blood itself is highly addictive**.
- **Mother Eve created the world managers.** One English line had the relationship backwards and made the world managers responsible for creating her.
- The Outlaw-blessing discussion says their suffering comes **not from Mother Eve abandoning them, but from punishment**. English changed that into "not only" abandonment plus punishment, reversing the contrast.
- Arthur's paralysis spell had already been removed and **Arthur is moving on her own**. English instead said she was still unable to move.
- In one Riche scene, **Riche works Aster's tongue between her lips**. The English version reversed the action and made Aster do it to her.
- The mirror-fragment side story had several direction errors in the same scene: **Natal rubs against Millet**, not the other way around, and the tail moves **from bottom to top**, not in the opposite direction.
- Mertel says **she** is about to climax. English turned the line into her telling Aster that it was time for **him** to climax.
- Aster asks Natal whether **she would be okay marrying someone like him**. English shifted the decision away from her.
- Ramius is ordered to **bring Riche back**. English reversed the request.
- A barricade scene says the armed people were probably **brought there against their will**. English invented a separate group of hostages instead.
- Croix's remaining soul residue is expected to **disappear eventually** now that her zombification has been cured. English changed this into a newly available procedure for removing it.
- The Arthur Tragedy history says **all of the Zero Knights serving at the time were killed**. English weakened the statement and changed the later succession details.
- One church demand is for **one hundred million Gold**, not one billion.
- Kath asks for **five more hours**, not five more minutes. The absurdly long request is the setup for the near-missed checkout joke.
- The Lancelot history says the republic split in two when **its water source dried up**. English turned that into Lancelot breaking away from Arthur "over water".
- Central prepared ships to **cross the ocean** during the Arthur Tragedy. English added the unsupported claim that the ships were built specifically to fight across it.
- Aster says he **will not accept the way Outlaws think**. English strengthened that into the much more extreme claim that he would sooner die than agree with them.
- One account of the shattered world has the speaker realize the earlier description **wasn't an exaggeration**. English reduced this to having thought it was merely a strange expression.
- The White Knight speaks from behind a **mask**, not a helmet.
- The English helper text confused two opposite battle properties: `暗殺有り` is **Can be assassinated**, while `捕獲は出来ません` is **Cannot be captured**.
- `斬られた騎士` is a **Slashed Knight**, not a **Slain Knight**; the character is still alive and reacting in the scene.
- A knight with an `鬼気迫る` expression looks **fierce**, not terrified.
- `退場願おうか` asks **the other person to leave**. One English line made the speaker leave instead.
- One scene says ordinary **tap water** would have worked as Kathryn's ingredient. English changed it to sewer water.
- Udon-tree **bark is processed into noodles**. English reversed the material relationship.
- The fake purifier is explicitly **not empty inside**. English said the opposite.
- Aster's epilogue refusal is something he is **thinking of doing** because succession is a possibility. English presented the refusal as already decided.
- One wedding exchange says the Church of Mother Eve teaches that **weddings should be solemn**, so the speaker will **do without** the troublesome cake. English reversed the conclusion into personally taking care of it.
- The floating-island explanation distinguishes magic that can **keep something afloat** from the much harder problem of making it **fly**. English blurred the two ideas together.
- A later line says the final two arrivals mean **the whole group is present**. The English version lost that completion point.
- Several not-X-but-Y constructions were reversed. Among them, Kathryn realizes **her face is flushed, not her hand warm**, and the women say **they**, not Aster, were the ones kept waiting.
- A stock `持病の癪` complaint is about **chronic stomach pains**. The English helper text changed it to **chronic hemorrhoids**, turning an old-fashioned complaint into an unrelated condition.

## Over-localized dialogue and invented material

- Rocinante's Japanese is deliberately odd, but the recurring **French gimmick** in the English release was invented. `ミー / ユー`, `イエース`, `サプラーイズ` and ordinary Japanese were turned into **moi, oui, bonjour, magnifique, sacre bleu** and similar French phrases that are not in the source.
- The same added French affected titles and names. A line even used **Monsieur Black Knight** where the Japanese has no French at all.
- Ramen was replaced with **pizza** in one joke and **tacos** in another. The second scene also lost the **charumera** ramen-vendor melody and reduced it to a generic bell.
- Croquettes became **pizza**, and a conversation about buying croquettes from **Nakamuraya**, including the typhoon/croquette joke, was rewritten into generic snacks.
- Aster's **onigiri and miso soup** became sandwiches and soup.
- A town known for **alcohol, miso and dried bonito** became a town known for **beer, yogurt and cheese**.
- The classic **Manju Kowai** setup was rewritten around **cake pops**.
- A joke about an old grandmother making unwanted piles of **tsukudani** became one about knitted sweaters and gained a new world-domination punchline.
- `ＭＨＫ（魔法放送協会）` was renamed **MBC (Magic Broadcasting Company)**, and one MHK joke was rewritten as a magazine-subscription gag. It is now **MHK (Magic Broadcasting Association)**, keeping the broadcaster parody.
- A 2D-otaku joke about preferring gay men in fiction acquired the modern English term **queer-baiting**, which is not present in the Japanese.
- A promise that breaking one's word means **a thousand needles** was replaced with "cross your heart and hope to die".
- Two self-correcting jokes, **punishment... I mean, education** and **candy... I mean, sex**, were replaced with a newly invented carrot-and-stick metaphor.
- A number of ordinary reactions gained English religious interjections such as **God** or **heaven** even though the Japanese contains none. These have been removed where they changed the imagery or tone.
- The English script frequently made neutral or mildly rough lines more vulgar. Added **fuck**, **harlot**, stronger insults and similar escalations were removed where the Japanese did not support them.
- Arthur's transformed-body material acquired a separate **"Slut Mode"** label in English. That concept is not in the Japanese.
- The Eden Item Shop Association was turned into a **union**.
- A plain marker (`マジック`) became the real-world brand **Sharpie**.
- Lancelot tourist statues picked up new slang such as **sweet peas**, **dudes and dudettes** and an invented recurring **hella cool guidance** line. Their actual odd greetings and local jokes are kept instead.
- Several English sayings were substituted for Japanese sayings even when the following joke depended on the original image. Those scenes now keep the Japanese proverb when the following joke still depends on its image.
- The recurring `ヒャッハー` cry became **Yahoo/Wahoo**, including item and weapon names. It is now **Hyahha**, matching the repeated source joke.
- `コノザマアタック` became the newly coined **Amuzattack**. **Konozama Attack** keeps the source `konozama` internet-slang reference.
- One NPC label `ロリ専ハニー` became **Pedo Hanny**. The label is now **Loli-only Hanny**, which is what the Japanese actually says.
- A Hanny stock trader's **"It's the IT age, you know."** was rewritten as **"What age are you living in?"**, changing a technology joke into an insult.
- Kathryn's melodramatic `諸行無常` while worrying about her breasts became **"It's all hopeless."** The restored line keeps the "all things are impermanent" joke.
- Ramius doing squats to raise the room's **humidity** for a sick Gurigura was rewritten as raising the room's **temperature**, breaking both the medical advice and the squat punchline.
- The recurring Alicesoft medicine `世色癌` is **Seirogan**. The English release renamed it **Ibeprofun** in dialogue, the item name, the use command and its battle description.
- **Hyakuen** and **Hyakuen Company** were renamed **Bigbux**, and the town's origin was rewritten around an invented lumberjack named Bigbux. In Japanese, the old lumber merchants named Hyakuen in the hope of forging **“a hundred bonds”** (`百の縁`).
- **High Tension Island** (`ハイテンション`) was renamed **Fervor** through most of the English script and UI, with **Frenzy** appearing in another lookup. The source place name is restored consistently.

## Jokes, references and wordplay

- `ＴＨＥ おしり！` keeps the deliberately English **THE butt!** instead of **Sweet ass!** Aster's `ＧＥＴだぜ！` likewise keeps the visible **GET** echo instead of flattening it to ordinary English.
- `ナイススイミング / ナイスタイミング` works again as **nice swimming / Nice timing**, followed by the question about where someone plans to swim. English changed the first half to **Perfect rhyming** and lost the punchline.
- `弱点を突くべし！　突くべし！` keeps the repeated **Strike the weak point! Strike!** cadence, preserving the *Ashita no Joe* echo.
- `だが断る！` is **But I refuse!**, keeping the recognizable *JoJo* phrasing rather than the generic **Rejected!**
- A dirty-room joke uses `腐海`, the **Sea of Corruption** from *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind*. English reduced it to the literal-sounding **sea of filth**.
- Riche quotes **“You cannot escape from the Great Demon King.”** The English release replaced the line with a **Babadook** joke, dropping the original reference entirely.
- Towa's father is **Rare Cheese** (`レアチーズ`). English renamed him **Laertes**, making the *Hamlet* connection more obvious at the cost of the game's deliberately stupid food-name joke; the restored script uses **Rare Cheese, Rare Cheese-san, Rare Cheese-sama** and **Rare Cheese-dono**.
- Donden Dodon's epitaph uses the familiar **“I'll get married when I get home”** death flag. English replaced it with the Western **one day from retirement** trope.
- A blue giant is compared to **Daidarabotchi**. English reduced the reference to a generic **titan**.
- Towa compares Aster's nearly bare face to a **Nopperabou**, and another joke invokes the **Oitekebori** after panties disappear unnoticed. English dropped the first youkai name and replaced the second joke with **“Am I possessed?”**
- A treasure-chest voice spoofs the familiar **Japanet** shopping jingle as **Shabunet**, then advertises meth. English renamed the spoof **Speednet**.
- The festival `ア / ク / ロ` **uchiwa fan** joke is restored: the kana held together spell **クロア (Croix)**. English replaced the fans and kana with Latin-letter signs.
- The natto/amanatto exchange again points out that both words contain the same **natto**. English reduced it to the vague claim that the names are merely similar.
- The **Yodel Onion** joke has two parts: the yodeling name and the later line that onions get the **tears flowing**.
- `潮 / 塩` is once again a **shio** homophone joke, with the salt misunderstanding followed by the sexual meaning. English had replaced it with a generic fluids/thirst exchange.
- `八方微塵 / 八方美人` is a near-sounding joke again, rendered as **Eight-Way Mincing / Eight-Way Mingling** rather than **Femme Fatality / femme fatale**.
- `呼ばれて飛び出てじゃじゃじゃじゃーーーん！` keeps the familiar *Hakushon Daimaou* cadence instead of becoming a generic **tadaaaaaah**.
- The Hanny parody of "the mountains rumbled and produced one mouse" again ends with **one Hanny** rather than replacing the source image.
- One `orz` joke had disappeared entirely. The Zero Knight is again described as crying in an **orz pose**, with enough context for the posture to make sense.
- `聖水 / 性水` again lands as the intended **holy water / sex water** pun.
- `必殺は必ず殺したいの略` is paired with **Hissatsu Sword** and keeps the joke that *hissatsu* is short for **"I definitely want to kill."**
- The Bigfoot plural joke deliberately uses the awkward **Bigfoots**, followed by **"That's hard to say."** English corrected it to **Bigfeet**, destroying the setup.
- The recurring **Dinner? A bath? Or me?** line is intact instead of being replaced by a different exchange.
- Aster's nervous marriage request again self-corrects from **Ramius...** to **your daughter...**
- Skilled Rangers are again said to be called **ninja**, which is what prompts the following **"Ninjas?!"** reaction.
- `見下しサンショウウオ` is **Condescending Salamander**: the joke is that the animal is literally above the party and figuratively looking down on them. **Watching Salamander** removed the punchline.
- A Hanny trap exchange again echoes itself: **"What a sneaky Hanny!" / "You called me sneaky!"** instead of two unrelated insults.
- The carbon-wood work chant is **Hei, hei, ho!**, and a `鉄砲` joke keeps **teppou** where the sound of the word itself is the point.
- Several battle barks keep source wordplay instead of generic attack lines, including the **brains / miso** joke and the deliberately odd **coarse chopping** cooking line.

## Food, culture and everyday details

- **Shio ramen** stays shio ramen; **cup yakisoba** is not reduced to cup noodles.
- **Haniwa soba** keeps its name and the joke that it uses **udon but is called soba**.
- A tourist comparison between **botamochi and ohagi** no longer becomes **pop and soda**.
- **Stir-fried mochi** is not a bagel saute, **Kashiwa Mochi** is not a sesame ball, and **bonito flakes** are not fish jerky.
- **Defense Manju / Stamina Manju** and **Dodgy Chazuke** keep their actual foods instead of being generalized to buns and soup.
- **Wakame-zake** is restored along with the follow-up joke about Riche being unable to do the "wakame" part because she has no hair down there. English had replaced the entire sequence with generic body shots and a joke about being too skinny.
- **Tempura set meal, anko, castella** and **ushi-mitsu manju** are kept instead of fish and chips, chocolate, sponge cake and "beef and honey bun."
- The Humpty menu again uses its odd names: **Eyeball Soba, Ketsune Soba, Bambara Chashu Ramen** and the **Daily Karaage Set**.
- Festival narration names **yakisoba** and **grilled beef skewers** instead of reducing everything to "a plethora of food."
- Two jokes specifically involve **aonori stuck in someone's teeth**, not generic bits of food or gunk.
- `花より団子` keeps the familiar **dumplings over flowers** image.
- `フライング甘食 / フライング辛食` are **Flying Amashoku / Flying Spicy Amashoku**, preserving the actual pastry and the pun.
- `ヤンキー` is used in the Japanese delinquent sense. The enemy and related jokes use **Delinquent**, not the misleading English **Yankee**.
- Cooking methods are no longer freely swapped. Soy-marinated, simmered and hotpot dishes remain those dishes instead of becoming saute, stir-fry or stew.
- `炊き込みご飯` is **seasoned rice**, and simmered radish remains simmered radish rather than becoming a meat-and-radish stir-fry.
- `鍋` dishes are described as **hotpot** where the scene is actually about hotpot.
- **Kemari** stays kemari rather than hackey sack; the surrounding sentence already makes clear that it is a ball game.
- A recovering Gurigura is brought **bread porridge and hot wine**, not porridge and tea.
- A first-period joke keeps **sekihan** instead of stating the period directly and dropping the red-rice custom.
- A coastal-town scene calls for **enka**, dried Squidman and **hot sake**. English replaced the image with a generic love song and going to a bar.
- **Sumo** stays sumo where the comparison specifically names it.
- `うみうし` is **Sea Slug / Giant Sea Slug**, not seacow.
- A **chikuwa** is a chikuwa, not a churro.
- A five-line shopping scene is about buying a **futon**, not a mattress.
- `利き酒` is **sake tasting**, and Humpty's `学習塾` is a **cram school**, not a band.
- One paperwork joke again requires the responsible person's **stamp** instead of changing the action into Aster signing the form himself.
- Erimo is seen playing **shogi and hanafuda** with the older people in Stallion. English changed them to chess and cards; another scene likewise restores **shogi, Iigo and beigoma** instead of chess, Othello and generic tops.
- Gurigura calls one exchange between Kathryn and Aster **married-couple manzai** (`夫婦漫才`), which Kathryn immediately denies. English rewrote it as a generic comedy-duo exchange and added its own “straight man” explanation.
- The recurring **herbivore / carnivore** (`草食系 / 肉食系`) dating metaphor was rewritten as **beta / alpha** language. That change also forced a later joke about “vegetarians” to be rewritten.
- One Hanny argument uses **yutori generation** as the insult. English substituted **millennial**, changing the generation and the cultural reference.
- The Nubobo Festival consistently calls Ramius a **miko** and her clothes a **miko outfit**. English alternated between **maiden** and **prophet**, even though the same game already uses **Momiji the Miko** elsewhere.
- Aster describes his own family home as **Japanese-style**. English changed this to **“your main house was more eastern-influenced,”** altering both the cultural description and **my** to **your**.
- Generic `酒 / お酒` is no longer automatically turned into beer or wine. Specific drinks remain specific where the Japanese names them, such as **fruit wine**, **hot sake** and **Akame wine**.

## Character voices and recurring terms

- Rocinante's `ミー / ユー` speech is visible again as **me / you / my / mine**, along with his deliberately English **time is money, yes, surprise** and similar lines.
- Riche's `アンダースタン？` and Aster's `イエース` exchange keeps the deliberate English **understand? / Y-Yes.** Aster's `マイ・サン` joke likewise remains **my son**.
- The `はい / イエス` wordplay is preserved as **hai / yes**, including the intentionally silly **Hai-yes!**
- Aster's `ギブミー` joke keeps **gimme**, and `問題ナッシング` keeps the deliberate **nothing** rather than replacing the whole expression with ordinary slang.
- Full English combat phrases that are already English in the Japanese script remain visibly that way, including **Go to hell!**, **Fuck you, bitch!** and the drawn-out **Fuck youuuuuuuu!**
- `ステルスマーケット` is **stealth marketing**, not viral marketing.
- `中二病`, `壁ドン` and `エロフラグ` remain **chuunibyou, kabedon** and **ero flag**. English had replaced all three with descriptive rewrites, erasing the repeated vocabulary.
- The recurring `寝取られ` joke stays **NTR**. English itself used “NTR scenario” once, but elsewhere rewrote the same term as **“stole my childhood friend,” “...What?”** or generic relationship language.
- **Yuri** stays yuri where the Japanese uses `百合` as a genre/trope term. English scattered the same joke across **“lesbian couple,” “into girls,” “female bonding”** and similar wording; recurring phrases such as **yuri couple** and **yuri vibrator** now line up again.
- **BL** stays BL. One Riche line had become **“dudes doing dudes,”** while another joke about replacing a heroine with a handsome man was rewritten as **“make it gayer.”**
- **Eroge** stays eroge, and the fictional **Ropper** computer is Ropper again instead of the real **X68000** substituted by the English release.
- `シスコン` is **siscon**; recurring otaku labels remain **otaku** rather than being rewritten as generic nerd descriptions. **Moe, lolicon/loli/shota, kotatsu, doujinshi, cosplay, NEET** and **otokonoko** likewise remain where those are the terms the game actually uses.
- The recurring `ラッキースケベ` joke uses **lucky sukebe** consistently. English stretched it into several different explanations, including **“implausible erotic situations,”** so the repeated joke stopped looking like a repeated term at all.
- `リア充` is consistently **normie**, preserving repeated jokes such as the normie aura and normies exploding instead of turning each line into unrelated jealousy or relationship talk.
- Story-convention vocabulary stays consistent: `フラグ` is a **flag**, `お約束` the **usual trope**, and `テンプレセリフ` a **cliche line**.
- Towa's `紙芝居` is **kamishibai** throughout her scenes instead of alternating among story, storytime, pictures and storytelling.
- Several `ボケ / ツッコミ` jokes keep the comedy structure with natural terms such as **comeback**, **straight man** and **manzai banter** where the scene needs them.
- `神童` is **child prodigy**, not the broader **savant**; a `シスター` is a **nun**, not a saint.
- The helper text keeps distinct roles where English had blurred them together, such as **hotel manager / branch manager**, **salesman**, **tavern keeper**, **senior adventurer** and **mercenary guard**.
- Source-neutral language is not automatically made harsher. Rough characters still sound rough, but ordinary surprise, frustration or sexual dialogue is no longer given extra profanity just to make it punchier.

## Honorifics and relationship language

- Gurigura normally calls Aster **Onii-chan**, or **Aster-oniichan** when she uses his name. She addresses his wives with the corresponding **-oneechan** forms, such as **Kath-oneechan, Riche-oneechan** and **Towa-oneechan**.
- Gurigura briefly switches from **Onii-chan** to **Onii-san** while imitating Kalar speech; the one-off register change is kept.
- Riche consistently uses **Onee-chan** for Croix instead of alternating among Croix, "my sister" and pronouns.
- Croix uses **Otouto-kun / Otouto** for Aster and sometimes calls herself **Onee-chan** while leaning into the older-sister role. The point where she drops the act and returns to calling him Aster is preserved too.
- Aster uses **Kyou-neechan, Kinou-neechan** and contextual **Nee-chan / Nee-chans**. Kyou also calls herself **Onee-chan** when playing up that role.
- Tilt calls Tio **Onee-chan**, and Sora/Mifa sister scenes keep the same relationship language where the Japanese uses it.
- Natal's **Onee-sama** for Millet is no longer localized as **Mistress**, which gave the relationship a master/servant tone that is not there in Japanese.
- Kinou and Sora keep their deliberate third-person self-reference where the Japanese has them use their own names instead of ordinary **I/me**. Millet does the same during her temporary childlike regression, while two degraded-state Beauty lines use **Beauty** for herself; these patterns are kept only in the scenes and states where the source actually uses them.
- Erimo also keeps the occasional playful **Onee-san** self-reference she uses when leaning into her older-woman role.
- Noa addresses an older schoolmate as **Senpai**. English changed both her line and his speaker label to **Chad**, replacing a relationship term with an internet archetype.
- Ramius calls Acheval **Aniue** throughout their family material and uses **Aneue** in the smaller set of respectful elder-sister references.
- Towa and Tio use **Aster-san** extensively. Laurent, Silent and Colpis also keep **Aster-san** even in intimate or sexual scenes, which is part of how their voices differ.
- Towa keeps **Erimo-san** as their relationship becomes closer; Aster similarly keeps **Silent-san, Sora-san, Mifa-san, Arthur-san** and other source forms where appropriate.
- **Aster-kun** is strongly associated with Sora and also appears from Barro, Felton, Tank, Palomides and others. **Edison-kun** and **Yamada-kun** are no longer Westernized to "Mr."
- Personal **-sama** forms are kept where the Japanese uses them. English replacements such as **Sir Aster, Lady/Mistress Natal, Princess Croix** and **Saint Silent** are not substituted for **Aster-sama, Natal-sama, Croix-sama** and **Silent-sama**.
- El Quixote's political and military dialogue keeps **-dono**, including forms such as **Barro-dono, Aster-dono, Pancho-dono** and **Rocinante-dono**.
- **Tio-sensei** and **Bolonius-sensei** are kept instead of routinely converting them to **Miss Tio** and **Dr. Bolonius**.
- A few gang members use **Aniki** for their leader; the hierarchy is no longer flattened to "man" or dropped entirely.
- Slurred or elongated forms remain relationship language too: drunken **Onee-sama**, calls of **Onii-chaaaan / Onee-chaaaan**, and Gurigura's drawn-out **Aniue** are not replaced with ordinary character names.

## UI, battle text and labels

- The live sort label `ＩＤ順` is **ID Order** instead of appearing in Japanese.
- Item and quest labels now agree with their descriptions: **Pasture Grass, Communicator, Pikari Ball** and **Skull Rock Fragment** are examples.
- Fishing and collection labels use the actual nouns, including **Millstone, Salmon Roe** and **Filefish**.
- `硫酸かけ` is **Sulfuric Acid Splash**, not the generic **Acid Splash**; `弓矢` is **Bow and Arrow**, keeping the bow that English omitted.
- Duplicate battle/helper aliases use the same wording as their visible counterparts, including **Assassination Immunity, Armor Recovery, Rapid Fire / Four-Shot Burst** and **Man-Eating**.
- Battle messages restore concrete wording where English had changed the meaning: `消し飛べ！` is **Get blown away!**, `これが神の怒りだぁあ！！` is **This is God's wrath!**, `えふえーっくす！！` is **F-X!!**, and `オイル漏れちゃった` is **My oil leaked!**
- Skill descriptions restore actual mechanics. **Status Cure** says it cures poison, shock and petrification; **First Aid** restores 25% of maximum HP; elemental Master skills add **Magic x0.3/x1.0** to spell power rather than claiming generic extra damage.
- **Fire Laser** and **Snow Laser** again explain what the F and S stand for.
- Music, chapter and location text was checked as well. Examples include **Perhaps an Old Story**, **Kindness and Heartache**, chapter 6's **Central in Turmoil**, and location descriptions that distinguish being **at** a landmark from merely being **near** it.
- Helper labels no longer invent character traits where the Japanese gives a plain role or state. Examples include **Earnest Kalar, Airheaded Poppin, Sycophant Hanny, Talkative Man, Delicate Girl, Puzzled Man** and **Wryly Smiling Knight**.
